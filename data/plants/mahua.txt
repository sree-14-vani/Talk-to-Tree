Common Name: Mahua / Butter Tree
Scientific Name: Madhuca longifolia
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Sapotaceae
Native Distribution: Central and North India; Nepal; Sri Lanka; Myanmar; Bangladesh
Habitat: Dry deciduous forests; plains; hills up to 1200m; tribal heartlands
Appearance: Large deciduous tree 17-20 meters; thick bark; clustered leaves at branch ends; creamy white fleshy fragrant flowers; fleshy berry 2-5 cm, greenish-yellow
Growth Characteristics: Moderate growth; long-lived; flowers Feb-Apr (leafless); fruits May-Jul; coppices well
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; drought tolerant; 800-1800mm rainfall; frost tolerant; USDA zones 9-11
Ecological Importance: Keystone species in dry forests; massive flower nectar; fruits for wildlife; tribal livelihood
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers attract bats, birds, insects; fruits eaten by deer, birds, tribal communities; host for insects
Cultural Historical Significance: "Mahua" central to tribal culture (Gond, Baiga, Santhal); flowers for liquor, food; "Tree of life" for tribals; mentioned in Ain-i-Akbari; economic mainstay
Interesting Facts: Flowers fermented for country liquor; edible flowers, fruits; seed oil for cooking, lighting; cake as fertilizer; "Mahua" = livelihood
Conservation Status: Not threatened; but habitat pressure
General Care: Minimal care; dry season essential; well-drained soil
Safety Notes: Fermented liquor - consume responsibly; do not use medicinally without expert guidance; seed oil edible but consult expert
