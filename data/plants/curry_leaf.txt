Common Name: Curry Leaf / Curry Tree
Scientific Name: Murraya koenigii
Plant Type: Tree / Shrub
Family: Rutaceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent; Sri Lanka; Bangladesh; Myanmar; Malaysia
Habitat: Tropical and subtropical forests; dry deciduous; plains to 1500m; often in undergrowth
Appearance: Small tree/shrub 4-6 meters; pinnate leaves 11-21 leaflets, aromatic; small white fragrant flowers; shiny black drupe 1 cm, 1-2 seeds
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; perennial; can be maintained as hedge; lives 20-30 years; coppices well
Environmental Requirements: Full sun to partial shade; well-drained soil; moderate water; frost sensitive; USDA zones 9-11
Ecological Importance: Understory species; nectar for pollinators; fruits for birds; aromatic foliage deters herbivores
Role in Biodiversity: Host for butterfly larvae (Citrus butterfly); flowers attract bees; fruits dispersed by birds
Cultural Historical Significance: "Kadi patta" essential in South Indian cuisine; mentioned in ancient Tamil literature; Ayurvedic medicinal plant; home garden staple
Interesting Facts: Not related to curry powder (spice blend); leaves lose flavor when dried; essential oil in perfumery; "sweet neem" (distinct from bitter neem); grows well in pots
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely cultivated
General Care: Regular harvesting promotes growth; well-drained soil; organic fertilizer; protect from frost; easily propagated from cuttings/seeds
Safety Notes: Generally safe as food; essential oil concentrated - use caution; do not use medicinally without expert guidance
