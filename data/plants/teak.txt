Common Name: Teak
Scientific Name: Tectona grandis
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Lamiaceae (formerly Verbenaceae)
Native Distribution: India, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos; naturalized in Africa, Central America, Indonesia
Habitat: Tropical deciduous forests; well-drained alluvial soils; 500-1000m elevation; distinct dry season
Appearance: Large deciduous tree 30-40 meters; straight trunk; large opposite leaves 30-60 cm, rough surface; small white fragrant flowers in panicles; globose drupe 1-2 cm in papery calyx
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing in youth; straight bole; lives 100+ years; ring-porous wood; deciduous in dry season
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; deep well-drained fertile soil; 1250-3750mm rainfall; dry season 3-5 months; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Dominant canopy species; leaf litter nutrient cycling; habitat for wildlife; carbon storage
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers for pollinators; fruits for birds; host for teak defoliator, skeletonizer; epiphytes on mature trees
Cultural Historical Significance: "King of timbers"; shipbuilding (British Navy); furniture, construction; mentioned in Arthashastra; teak plantations since 1840s (Nilambur); Myanmar teak famous
Interesting Facts: Natural oils make water/termite/fungus resistant; silica dulls tools; oldest teak plantation 1842; "teak" from Malayalam "tekka"; golden brown heartwood
Conservation Status: Not threatened (cultivated); wild populations endangered (IUCN); illegal logging concern
General Care: Weed control critical first 3 years; thinning at 5, 10, 15 years; fire protection; no fertilizer needed
Safety Notes: Sawdust may cause dermatitis; do not consume any part
