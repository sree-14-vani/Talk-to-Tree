Common Name: Neem / Margosa / Indian Lilac
Scientific Name: Azadirachta indica
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Meliaceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent, Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, Iran, Pakistan
Habitat: Tropical and subtropical dry deciduous forests; plains and low hills; semi-arid regions; often naturalized in Africa, Americas, Australia
Appearance: Medium to large evergreen tree 15-20 meters tall; straight trunk with rough fissured bark; pinnate compound leaves 20-40 cm long with 8-19 leaflets; small white fragrant flowers in panicles; olive-like drupe fruit 1-2 cm long, yellow-green when ripe
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; drought tolerant; deep taproot system; lives 150-200 years; evergreen but may shed leaves in severe drought; coppices well
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained sandy or loamy soil; tolerates poor, rocky, saline, alkaline soils; pH 5-10; very drought tolerant once established; frost sensitive; USDA zones 9-11
Ecological Importance: Pioneer species for degraded land restoration; nitrogen-fixing associations; nectar source for bees and pollinators; fruits eaten by birds and bats; leaf litter enriches soil; windbreak and shade tree
Role in Biodiversity: Host plant for various butterfly species; flowers attract honeybees; fruits dispersed by birds (bulbuls, mynas, parakeets) and bats; supports insect diversity
Cultural Historical Significance: "Village pharmacy" in Indian tradition; mentioned in ancient Ayurvedic texts (Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita); used in traditional medicine for 4000+ years; sacred in some Hindu traditions; planted near homes for protection
Interesting Facts: All parts have medicinal properties; azadirachtin from seeds is potent natural insecticide; neem oil used in cosmetics, soaps, agriculture; twigs traditionally used as toothbrush (datun); wood termite-resistant; "Sarva Roga Nivarini" (curator of all ailments) in Sanskrit
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely cultivated globally; some wild populations declining due to habitat loss
General Care: Minimal care once established; water regularly first year; prune to shape; easily grown from fresh seeds; can be propagated by cuttings
Safety Notes: Neem oil can be toxic if ingested in large quantities; do not use during pregnancy; may interact with medications; do not consume any part without expert guidance; consult appropriate expert before medicinal use
