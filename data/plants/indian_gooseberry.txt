Common Name: Indian Gooseberry / Amla
Scientific Name: Phyllanthus emblica
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Phyllanthaceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent; Sri Lanka; Southeast Asia; southern China
Habitat: Tropical and subtropical dry deciduous forests; plains; hills up to 1500m; often near streams
Appearance: Small to medium deciduous tree 8-18 meters; feathery pinnate leaves; greenish-yellow flowers in axillary clusters; spherical pale green fruit 2-3 cm, 6 vertical striations
Growth Characteristics: Moderate growth; fruits in 4-5 years; productive 60-70 years; deep root system; tolerates alkalinity
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; tolerates saline/alkaline; drought tolerant; light frost tolerant; USDA zones 9-11
Ecological Importance: Pioneer species; fruits for wildlife; soil improvement; watershed protection
Role in Biodiversity: Fruits eaten by birds, mammals; flowers for pollinators; host for insects
Cultural Historical Significance: "Amalaki" in Sanskrit; sacred in Hinduism (Vishnu, Lakshmi); Chyawanprash base; mentioned in Charaka Samhita; tree worshipped on Amalaki Ekadashi; "Amritphala" (fruit of immortality)
Interesting Facts: Highest natural vitamin C content (600-700mg/100g); fruit remains fresh months; all parts used; wood for furniture; tannin from bark; ink from fruit
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely cultivated
General Care: Regular watering when young; organic manure; prune for light penetration; harvest winter
Safety Notes: High acidity - consume moderation; may interact with medications; do not use medicinally without expert guidance
