Common Name: Pongam / Indian Beech / Karanja
Scientific Name: Millettia pinnata (formerly Pongamia pinnata)
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Fabaceae
Native Distribution: Tropical and temperate Asia; India, Bangladesh, Myanmar, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines, Australia, Pacific Islands
Habitat: Coastal areas; tidal forests; riverbanks; lowlands; tolerates salinity; up to 1200m
Appearance: Medium evergreen tree 15-25 meters; broad canopy; pinnate leaves 5-7 leaflets; pink-white pea-like flowers in racemes; flat elliptical pods 4-5 cm, 1-2 seeds
Growth Characteristics: Moderate growth; nitrogen-fixing; lives 50-100 years; coppices well; salt tolerant
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; tolerates saline, waterlogged; 500-2500mm rainfall; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-11
Ecological Importance: Nitrogen fixation; coastal stabilization; mangrove associate; shade; nectar; biodiesel potential
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers for bees; pods for wildlife; host for lac insects; mangrove ecosystem component
Cultural Historical Significance: "Karanja" in Sanskrit; oil for lamps, medicine; Ayurvedic for skin; biodiesel research; shade for coffee
Interesting Facts: Seeds 30-40% oil (pongamia oil); non-edible biodiesel candidate; cake as fertilizer; "Karanja" oil in traditional lamps
Conservation Status: Not threatened
General Care: Minimal care; salt tolerant; prune for shape; seeds for propagation
Safety Notes: Seeds toxic if ingested; oil not for consumption; do not use without expert guidance
