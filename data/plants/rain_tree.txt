Common Name: Rain Tree / Monkeypod / Saman
Scientific Name: Samanea saman (formerly Albizia saman)
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
Native Distribution: Central America, Northern South America, Caribbean; widely naturalized in tropics
Habitat: Tropical dry and moist forests; savannas; roadsides; pastures; up to 1000m
Appearance: Large deciduous tree 20-30 meters; massive spreading canopy (30+ meters wide); bipinnate leaves fold at night/rain; pink-white powderpuff flowers; long flat black pods 15-20 cm
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; semi-deciduous; lives 80-100 years; nitrogen-fixing; shallow buttress roots
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; drought tolerant; 600-3000mm rainfall; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Nitrogen fixation; massive shade reduces soil temperature; leaf litter enriches soil; carbon sequestration; fodder from pods/leaves
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers attract bats, bees, birds; pods eaten by livestock, wildlife; canopy hosts epiphytes; host for lac insects
Cultural Historical Significance: "Rain tree" - leaves fold before rain; introduced to Sri Lanka 1800s; avenue tree in Southeast Asia; "Saman" in Sanskrit; Simon Bolivar camped under one; shade for coffee/cocoa plantations
Interesting Facts: Leaves fold at night (nyctinasty) and before rain; canopy can cover 1500+ sq meters; pods sweet pulp edible; wood for carvings; "monkeypod" - monkeys eat pods
Conservation Status: Not threatened; invasive in some Pacific islands
General Care: Ample space for canopy; minimal water once established; prune low branches; nitrogen-fixing - no N fertilizer needed
Safety Notes: Pods edible but do not consume large quantities without expert guidance
