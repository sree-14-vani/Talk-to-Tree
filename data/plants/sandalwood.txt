Common Name: Sandalwood / Indian Sandalwood
Scientific Name: Santalum album
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Santalaceae
Native Distribution: Peninsular India (Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala); Sri Lanka; Indonesia; Malaysia; Australia
Habitat: Dry deciduous forests; rocky hills; well-drained lateritic soils; 600-1000m elevation
Appearance: Small to medium evergreen tree 4-9 meters; slender drooping branches; opposite ovate leaves 3-5 cm; small purplish flowers; globose drupe 1 cm, black when ripe
Growth Characteristics: Slow-growing; hemiparasitic (roots attach to host plants for water/nutrients); matures 15-20 years for heartwood; lives 60-100 years
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained sandy/rocky soil; 600-1600mm rainfall; host plants required (Acacia, Casuarina, etc.); frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-11
Ecological Importance: Hemiparasitic relationships create unique plant communities; fruits dispersed by birds; supports specialized insects
Role in Biodiversity: Host-specific root parasites; fruits eaten by birds (bulbuls, mynas); specialized sandalwood spike disease vector
Cultural Historical Significance: Sacred in Hinduism, Buddhism; "Chandan" used in rituals, incense, paste; mentioned in Vedas, Ramayana; Mysore sandalwood famous; royal monopoly historically; carved artifacts, temples
Interesting Facts: Heartwood forms only after 15+ years; oil from heartwood (not bark/leaves); one of world's most expensive woods; hemiparasite - needs host but photosynthesizes; spike disease devastated plantations
Conservation Status: Vulnerable (IUCN); overharvesting, habitat loss, spike disease; strict regulation in India; plantation efforts ongoing
General Care: Requires host plants; minimal water once established; no fertilizer; protect from spike disease; long gestation
Safety Notes: Essential oil potent - use diluted; do not ingest oil without expert guidance; consult appropriate expert
