Common Name: Bel / Wood Apple / Bengal Quince
Scientific Name: Aegle marmelos
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Rutaceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent; Sri Lanka; Myanmar; Thailand; naturalized in Southeast Asia
Habitat: Dry deciduous forests; plains; hills up to 1200m; tolerates arid conditions
Appearance: Medium deciduous tree 10-15 meters; trifoliate aromatic leaves; greenish-white fragrant flowers; large globose woody fruit 5-12 cm, hard shell, aromatic pulp
Growth Characteristics: Slow-growing; very drought tolerant; lives 50+ years; spiny branches; deep roots
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; very drought tolerant; tolerates alkaline; frost tolerant; USDA zones 9-11
Ecological Importance: Dry forest component; fruits for wildlife in lean season; nectar for bees; soil stabilization
Role in Biodiversity: Fruits eaten by elephants, deer, birds; flowers for pollinators; host for butterfly larvae
Cultural Historical Significance: "Bilva" sacred to Shiva; trifoliate leaf = trident; offered in Shiva worship; mentioned in Skanda Purana; fruit in rituals; "Shiva's tree"
Interesting Facts: Fruit shell so hard needs hammer; pulp eaten fresh, sherbet, jam; leaves for diabetes (traditional); wood for agricultural tools; marmelosin compound
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely cultivated near temples
General Care: Minimal water once established; well-drained; protect from waterlogging
Safety Notes: Fruit pulp safe; do not consume leaves/bark medicinally without expert guidance
