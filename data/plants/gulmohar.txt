Common Name: Gulmohar / Flamboyant / Flame Tree
Scientific Name: Delonix regia
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
Native Distribution: Madagascar (endemic); widely naturalized in tropics worldwide
Habitat: Tropical dry deciduous forests; coastal regions; disturbed sites; up to 1000m elevation
Appearance: Deciduous tree 10-15 meters tall; broad umbrella-shaped canopy; bipinnate feathery leaves 30-50 cm; spectacular orange-red flowers 8 cm across in clusters; long flat woody pods 30-60 cm
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; semi-deciduous in dry season; lives 50-60 years; shallow root system; nitrogen-fixing
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; drought tolerant once established; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Nitrogen fixation enriches soil; nectar for bees, birds; shade tree; ornamental; erosion control
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers attract sunbirds, hummingbirds, bees; pods eaten by some birds; host for butterfly larvae
Cultural Historical Significance: Introduced to India by British in 1800s; "Gulmohar" = "flower of the peacock" (Persian); avenue tree in Indian cities; summer flowering icon; planted in schools, parks
Interesting Facts: One of world's most colorful flowering trees; pods used as percussion instruments; wood lightweight; bark used in tanning; threatened in native Madagascar
Conservation Status: Endangered in native Madagascar (IUCN); widely cultivated globally; not threatened elsewhere
General Care: Full sun essential; minimal water once established; prune after flowering; protect from strong winds (shallow roots)
Safety Notes: Seeds toxic if ingested in large quantities; do not consume any part without expert guidance
