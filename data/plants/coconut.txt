Common Name: Coconut
Scientific Name: Cocos nucifera
Plant Type: Palm
Family: Arecaceae
Native Distribution: Tropical coastlines of Indian and Pacific Oceans; exact origin debated (Indo-Pacific)
Habitat: Sandy coastal soils; tropical beaches; tolerates salinity; up to 1000m elevation near coast
Appearance: Tall single-stemmed palm 20-30 meters; crown of pinnate fronds 4-6 meters long; fibrous husk covering large seed (coconut); inflorescence spadix with male/female flowers
Growth Characteristics: Slow initial growth; fruits in 6-10 years; productive 60-80 years; no taproot - fibrous root system; wind resistant; salt spray tolerant
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; sandy well-drained soil; high humidity; 1500-2500mm rainfall; mean temp 27°C; frost intolerant; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Coastal stabilization; habitat for specialized fauna; nutrient cycling in atoll ecosystems; pioneer on new islands
Role in Biodiversity: Specific pollinators (weevils, bees); fruits dispersed by ocean currents; epiphytes on trunks; coconuts food for coconut crab, rodents
Cultural Historical Significance: "Tree of life" (Kalpa Vriksha) in Sanskrit; every part used; ancient maritime dispersal by Austronesians; sacred in Hindu rituals; copra trade shaped colonial economies
Interesting Facts: Botanically a drupe, not a nut; coconut water is sterile when fresh; coir from husk used for ropes/mats; shell for charcoal/crafts; "coconut" from Portuguese "coco" (grinning face)
Conservation Status: Not threatened; genetic diversity threatened by monoculture; lethal yellowing disease affects some regions
General Care: Regular watering when young; salt-tolerant fertilizer; remove dead fronds; protect from rhinoceros beetle
Safety Notes: Falling coconuts cause injuries; do not consume fermented coconut water; consult expert for medicinal uses
