Common Name: Kadamba / Burflower Tree
Scientific Name: Neolamarckia cadamba
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Rubiaceae
Native Distribution: South and Southeast Asia; India, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Sri Lanka, China, Malaysia, Indonesia, Philippines
Habitat: Tropical moist deciduous forests; riverbanks; lowland areas; up to 1000m; prefers moist soil
Appearance: Large deciduous tree 20-45 meters; straight trunk; simple opposite leaves 13-32 cm; orange-yellow fragrant globular flower heads; small fleshy capsules in clusters
Growth Characteristics: Very fast-growing; straight bole; lives 50-100 years; pioneers open areas; buttress roots
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; moist well-drained soil; high rainfall 1500-4000mm; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Rapid soil improvement; nectar for bats, bees; fruits for birds, bats; shade for understory; watershed protection
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers pollinated by bats; fruits eaten by birds, bats, primates; host for insects; cavity nester habitat
Cultural Historical Significance: "Kadamba" associated with Krishna (Kadamba forest); favorite of Durga; mentioned in Sanskrit poetry; Kadamba dynasty named after tree; flowers in festivals
Interesting Facts: Flowers bloom at night; strong fragrance at dusk; "Kadamba" in ancient Tamil literature; wood for plywood, paper; flowers for perfume
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely planted
General Care: Ample water; space for growth; minimal pruning; grown from seeds
Safety Notes: Do not consume without expert guidance
