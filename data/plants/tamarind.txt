Common Name: Tamarind
Scientific Name: Tamarindus indica
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
Native Distribution: Tropical Africa; naturalized Indian subcontinent, Southeast Asia, Americas
Habitat: Tropical dry deciduous forests; savannas; coastal areas; up to 1500m; semi-arid regions
Appearance: Large evergreen tree 20-25 meters; dense spreading crown; pinnate leaves 10-20 leaflets; pale yellow flowers with red veins; curved brown pods 10-15 cm, sticky pulp
Growth Characteristics: Slow-growing; long-lived (200+ years); deep taproot; very drought tolerant; salt tolerant
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; tolerates poor/alkaline/saline; 500-1500mm rainfall; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-11
Ecological Importance: Shade tree in arid regions; nitrogen fixation (controversial); soil stabilization; fodder; nectar source
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers for bees; pods for wildlife; canopy habitat; host for lac insects
Cultural Historical Significance: "Imli" in Hindi; ancient trade commodity; mentioned in Indian texts; used in cuisines worldwide; Worcestershire sauce ingredient; traditional medicine
Interesting Facts: "Date of India" (tamar hindi); pulp high tartaric acid; seeds yield pectin; wood hard, durable; leaves edible; pulp polishes brass/copper
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely naturalized
General Care: Minimal care once established; deep watering; prune for structure; harvest pods dry
Safety Notes: High acid content - dental erosion risk; may interact with medications; do not consume medicinally without expert guidance
