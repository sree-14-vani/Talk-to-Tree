Common Name: Semal / Silk Cotton Tree / Red Silk Cotton
Scientific Name: Bombax ceiba
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Malvaceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent; Southeast Asia; southern China; northern Australia
Habitat: Tropical and subtropical dry deciduous forests; riverbanks; plains; up to 1500m
Appearance: Large deciduous tree 20-30 meters; massive trunk with conical spines when young; digitately compound leaves; spectacular red/orange cup-shaped flowers 7-11 cm; large capsules with silky cotton fibers
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; leafless when flowering (Feb-Apr); lives 100+ years; buttress roots; spines disappear with age
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained soil; distinct dry season; drought tolerant; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Massive nectar source for birds, bats; cotton fibers for bird nests; pioneer species; soil stabilization
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers attract parrots, mynas, sunbirds, bats; nectar feast; fruits for wildlife; host for insects
Cultural Historical Significance: "Semal" in Hindi; associated with Holi festival; mentioned in Ramayana; cotton for stuffing pillows; wood for matchsticks, packing cases
Interesting Facts: Flowers before leaves; "cotton tree" - fibers not true cotton; one of first to flower in spring; spines protect young tree; "King of forest flowers"
Conservation Status: Not threatened
General Care: Dry season essential for flowering; well-drained soil; minimal water once established
Safety Notes: Do not consume without expert guidance; spines on young trees
