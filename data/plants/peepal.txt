Common Name: Peepal / Sacred Fig / Bodhi Tree
Scientific Name: Ficus religiosa
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Moraceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Southwest China, Indochina, Peninsular Malaysia
Habitat: Tropical and subtropical regions; riverbanks, plains, foothills; often near temples and settlements; epiphytic in early life on other trees
Appearance: Large deciduous/semi-evergreen tree 20-30 meters tall; heart-shaped leaves 10-17 cm long with distinctive long tapering tip (drip tip); smooth pale gray bark; small figs in pairs, purple when ripe; young leaves pinkish-bronze
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; long-lived (several hundred years); can start as epiphyte then send down roots; develops buttress roots at base; canopy spreading
Environmental Requirements: Full sun to partial shade; well-drained soil; tolerates variety of soils; moderate drought tolerance; prefers warm humid climate; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Keystone fig species; fruits available year-round supporting frugivores; fig-wasp mutualism; canopy provides nesting and roosting; roots stabilize riverbanks; carbon sequestration
Role in Biodiversity: Critical food source for birds (hornbills, barbets, pigeons, bulbuls), bats, primates; specific pollinator wasp (Blastophaga quadraticeps); hosts epiphytes
Cultural Historical Significance: Sacred in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism; Buddha attained enlightenment under Bodhi tree (Ficus religiosa); mentioned in Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita; symbol of enlightenment, longevity; village deity tree; circumambulation ritual
Interesting Facts: Leaves move continuously even without wind due to long petiole; "Bodhi tree" at Bodh Gaya is descendant of original; releases oxygen at night (CAM photosynthesis); leaf shape inspired architectural motifs
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely protected due to religious significance
General Care: Sacred tree - often not pruned; requires space; water when young; grown from seeds or cuttings
Safety Notes: Latex may cause skin irritation; do not consume without expert guidance; fruits generally safe when ripe but consult expert
