Common Name: Jamun / Java Plum / Indian Blackberry
Scientific Name: Syzygium cumini
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Myrtaceae
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent; Myanmar; Sri Lanka; Southeast Asia; naturalized in tropics worldwide
Habitat: Tropical and subtropical forests; riverbanks; moist valleys; up to 1800m; often near water
Appearance: Large evergreen tree 20-30 meters; dense canopy; opposite glossy leaves 5-12 cm; fragrant white flowers in clusters; oblong drupe 1.5-3.5 cm, purple-black when ripe
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; long-lived (100+ years); fruits in 6-8 years; deep root system; shade tolerant when young
Environmental Requirements: Full sun to partial shade; moist well-drained soil; tolerates waterlogging; frost sensitive; USDA zones 9-11
Ecological Importance: Riverbank stabilization; massive fruit crop for wildlife; year-round canopy cover; carbon sequestration
Role in Biodiversity: Fruits eaten by birds (hornbills, bulbuls, parakeets), bats, mammals; flowers for bees; host for insects
Cultural Historical Significance: "Jambu" in Sanskrit; associated with Lord Krishna; mentioned in Ramayana; "Jambudvipa" (continent) named after it; fruit stains purple; wine, vinegar from fruit
Interesting Facts: Fruit lowers blood sugar (traditional use); seeds used in Ayurveda; bark for tanning; wood water-resistant; flowers at night
Conservation Status: Not threatened; widely cultivated
General Care: Regular watering; organic mulch; prune for shape; harvest ripe fruits
Safety Notes: Seeds may lower blood sugar - caution with diabetes medication; do not use medicinally without expert guidance
