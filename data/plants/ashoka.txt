Common Name: Ashoka
Scientific Name: Saraca asoca (also Saraca indica)
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Fabaceae (Leguminosae)
Native Distribution: Indian subcontinent (central, eastern, western India); Sri Lanka; Myanmar; Malaysia
Habitat: Tropical evergreen forests; stream banks; shaded ravines; up to 750m elevation
Appearance: Small evergreen tree 7-10 meters; dense spreading crown; paripinnate leaves with 6-12 leaflets; fragrant orange-yellow flowers in dense clusters; flat woody pods 10-20 cm
Growth Characteristics: Slow-growing; shade-loving in youth; lives 50+ years; coppices well
Environmental Requirements: Partial shade to full sun; moist well-drained soil; high humidity; 1500-3000mm rainfall; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-11
Ecological Importance: Understory species in evergreen forests; nectar for pollinators; soil stabilization along streams
Role in Biodiversity: Flowers attract bees, butterflies, birds; host for butterfly larvae; fruits dispersed by birds
Cultural Historical Significance: "Ashoka" = "without sorrow" (Sanskrit); sacred in Hinduism, Buddhism; Buddha born under Ashoka tree (Lumbini); Sita held captive in Ashoka grove (Ramayana); Kamadeva's arrow flowers; planted in temples, gardens
Interesting Facts: Flowers bloom February-April; bark used in Ayurveda; "Ashoka Shatakam" Sanskrit poem; national flower of Madhya Pradesh; distinct from Polyalthia longifolia (false ashoka)
Conservation Status: Vulnerable (IUCN); habitat loss, overharvesting for bark; protected in reserves
General Care: Shade when young; regular moisture; organic mulch; minimal pruning
Safety Notes: Bark used medicinally but do not self-medicate; consult appropriate expert
