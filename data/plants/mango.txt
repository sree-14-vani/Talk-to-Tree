Common Name: Mango
Scientific Name: Mangifera indica
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Anacardiaceae
Native Distribution: South Asia (India, Myanmar, Bangladesh); cultivated worldwide in tropics/subtropics
Habitat: Tropical lowlands; well-drained plains; foothills up to 1200m; requires dry season for flowering
Appearance: Large evergreen tree 15-30 meters tall; dense rounded canopy; simple lanceolate leaves 15-35 cm long, pinkish when young; small fragrant yellowish flowers in panicles; large drupe fruit variable size/shape/color
Growth Characteristics: Long-lived (300+ years); deep taproot; alternate bearing tendency; grafted trees fruit in 3-5 years; canopy spreads 10-15 meters
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; well-drained deep soil; pH 5.5-7.5; distinct dry season for flowering; frost sensitive; 300-2500mm rainfall; USDA zones 10-11
Ecological Importance: Fruit for wildlife (birds, bats, mammals); flowers for pollinators; shade tree; leaf litter enriches soil; windbreak
Role in Biodiversity: Host for various insects; fruits eaten by hornbills, parakeets, flying foxes; flowers attract bees, wasps
Cultural Historical Significance: "King of fruits" in India; national fruit of India, Pakistan, Philippines; cultivated 4000+ years; mentioned in Hindu texts, Buddhist Jatakas; Mughal emperors established orchards; mango leaves used in rituals, decorations
Interesting Facts: Over 1000 cultivars; largest fruit recorded 3.4kg; alternate bearing (heavy crop alternate years); mango wood used for furniture; kernel oil in cosmetics; international mango festival in Delhi
Conservation Status: Not threatened; wild relatives threatened by habitat loss; many landraces endangered
General Care: Regular watering when young; fertilizer for fruiting; prune after harvest; protect from frost; grafted varieties preferred
Safety Notes: Sap and peel contain urushiol (like poison ivy) - may cause contact dermatitis; do not consume leaves/bark without expert guidance; mango kernel toxic in large amounts
