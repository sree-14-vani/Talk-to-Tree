Common Name: Jackfruit
Scientific Name: Artocarpus heterophyllus
Plant Type: Tree
Family: Moraceae
Native Distribution: Western Ghats, India; Bangladesh; Sri Lanka; Malaysia; Indonesia
Habitat: Tropical lowland forests; humid hillsides; up to 1500m; cultivated throughout tropics
Appearance: Large evergreen tree 15-20 meters; dense canopy; simple glossy leaves 10-15 cm; cauliflorous flowers on trunk/main branches; massive syncarp fruit 30-90 cm, 10-50 kg, greenish-yellow spiny rind
Growth Characteristics: Fast-growing; fruits in 3-4 years (grafted); productive 50+ years; deep taproot; latex in all parts
Environmental Requirements: Full sun; deep well-drained soil; high humidity; 1500-2500mm rainfall; frost sensitive; USDA zones 10-12
Ecological Importance: Massive fruit feeds numerous animals; year-round flowering/fruiting; canopy habitat; carbon sequestration
Role in Biodiversity: Fruits eaten by elephants, deer, monkeys, bats, birds; seeds dispersed by mammals; flowers pollinated by insects, wind; epiphyte host
Cultural Historical Significance: National fruit of Bangladesh; "Panasa" in Sanskrit texts; cultivated 3000+ years; staple food in regions; wood for furniture, instruments; leaves for wrapping
Interesting Facts: Largest tree-borne fruit; 100-500 seeds per fruit; unripe fruit used as vegetable/meat substitute; seeds edible roasted; wood termite-resistant; latex for birdlime
Conservation Status: Not threatened; wild relatives threatened; many cultivars
General Care: Regular watering; mulch; fertilizer for fruiting; protect young from frost; prune for shape
Safety Notes: Latex causes contact dermatitis; raw seeds toxic - must cook; do not consume without proper preparation; consult expert
