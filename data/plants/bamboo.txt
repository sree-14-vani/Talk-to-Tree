Common Name: Bamboo
Scientific Name: Bambusa vulgaris (Common Bamboo) / Dendrocalamus strictus (Male Bamboo) / various species
Plant Type: Grass (Giant Woody Grass)
Family: Poaceae (Gramineae)
Native Distribution: Tropical and subtropical Asia, Africa, Americas; widely cultivated globally
Habitat: Riverbanks, forest edges, hillsides; moist well-drained soils; from sea level to 3000m depending on species
Appearance: Culms (stems) jointed, hollow between nodes; 10-30 meters tall depending on species; diameter 5-30 cm; rhizomatous root system; lanceolate leaves; flowering rare (gregarious, 30-120 year cycles)
Growth Characteristics: Fastest growing woody plants (up to 91 cm/day); matures in 3-5 years; spreads via rhizomes (running) or clumps (clumping); dies after flowering (monocarpic)
Environmental Requirements: Full sun to partial shade; moist well-drained soil; high humidity; species-specific temperature ranges; USDA zones 5-11 depending on species
Ecological Importance: Carbon sequestration (35% more than trees); soil erosion control; watershed protection; rapid biomass production; phytoremediation
Role in Biodiversity: Habitat for pandas, lemurs, birds, insects; specific bamboo-dependent species; shoots food for herbivores; leaf litter unique decomposition
Cultural Historical Significance: "Poor man's timber" in Asia; construction, furniture, paper, crafts; musical instruments (flute, angklung); writing material (bamboo slips); symbol of resilience, flexibility in Asian philosophy
Interesting Facts: Grass not wood; tensile strength rivals steel; some species flower synchronously worldwide; bamboo flowering followed by mass die-off; used in scaffolding skyscrapers in Hong Kong; edible shoots
Conservation Status: Some species threatened by habitat loss; overharvesting; flowering die-offs; many species in cultivation
General Care: Contain running species with barriers; regular watering; thin clumps; harvest mature culms (3+ years); fertilize with nitrogen
Safety Notes: Raw shoots contain cyanogenic glycosides - must be cooked thoroughly; do not consume without proper preparation; some species invasive
